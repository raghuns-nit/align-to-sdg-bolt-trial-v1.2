SCOPE This specification describes the intended final-state system: ontology, graph, semantic 
retrieval, recommendation reasoning, educator-input routing, educational-framework enrichment, 
provenance, administration, deployment and research evaluation. Notebook execution and low-level 
debugging procedures remain outside scope; validation-informed design evolution is included where 
it materially explains a final-state architectural choice.

1. Research problem and system objective
Higher-education curricula increasingly need to address sustainability, but the integration 
problem is not simply a matter of attaching SDG labels to course topics. A technical topic can 
support different sustainability implications depending on the application, operational action, 
affected resource, stakeholder setting and boundary conditions. Conversely, many technical 
topics have no defensible sustainability implication in a particular teaching instance. The 
system therefore treats sustainability integration as a constrained semantic reasoning problem 
rather than a keyword-matching or unconstrained text-generation problem.
The final-state system is designed to assist educators in discovering, justifying and translating sustainability 
relevance within existing curricula. It combines a governed ontology, a Neo4j knowledge graph, semantic 
embeddings, controlled graph traversal, curated Application Context evidence, discipline guardrails and 
educational-framework references. Its output is an explainable educator-facing recommendation bundle, not 
merely an SCR label.
RESEARCH PROBLEM How can an intelligent system recommend sustainability integration 
opportunities from discipline-specific curriculum content while remaining technically valid, context￾sensitive, cross-disciplinary, explainable and educationally actionable?

2. Design invariants
• Ontology and curated typed relationships are the semantic authority. Embeddings are a retrieval layer, not 
a source of ontology truth.
• KCR, KPR, KFR and SCR are independent repositories. KPR and KFR connect to KCR through typed 
relationships; they are not mandatory sequential levels.
• The selected Subtopic is the preferred technical scope. Module and Course are controlled fallback scopes; 
unrelated siblings are not silently imported.
• Prerequisite relationships inform readiness/preparation, not topical relevance.
• Application Context may create sustainability relevance only when it is compatible with the technical 
anchor and boundary conditions.
• Educator-entered Application Context supersedes system-generated context for the primary 
recommendation path; system-generated alternatives remain explicitly secondary.
• Guardrails constrain unsupported technical inference. They do not prohibit a valid cross-domain 
sustainability concept simply because its sustainability dimension differs from the course discipline.
• Eligibility is evaluated before ranking. Ranking compares only candidates that survive technical, 
mechanism, boundary and guardrail checks.
• SDG/ESD/Curriculum Framework records enrich a sustainability recommendation only after SCR eligibility 
is established.
• TopicSemanticProfile and ApplicationMechanismProfile are transient runtime projections. 
RecommendationEvent and RecommendationContextSnapshot are persisted for replay and audit.
• Repository entity embeddings are generated and persisted; query vectors and similarity scores are request￾specific. Scores become persistent only as part of recommendation provenance.
• Feedback informs review, calibration and curation proposals; it MUST NOT silently rewrite canonical 
ontology entities or relationships.
SLIDE TAKEAWAY A single principle governs the design: retrieval may suggest, but only 
governed evidence may justify.
• When a curriculum anchor is explicitly selected, it remains authoritative for technical scope. Educator￾entered Topic/Context/Question text may change application evidence but MUST NOT silently replace that 
anchor.
• Educator-entered Context/Question is preserved verbatim for provenance and normalized into transient 
mechanism facets for runtime reasoning; the normalized profile does not overwrite the original input.
• System-generated Application Context alternatives MUST pass technical/mechanism/boundary compatibility 
before semantic ranking. When no direct structural match exists, controlled semantic fallback MAY be used.
• If only free technical text is supplied, the system MUST resolve a defensible ontology anchor before SCR 
recommendation; ambiguous or low-confidence resolution is a valid block/clarification outcome.

5.1 KCR/KPR/KFR are not a chain
A frequent modelling error is to read KCR KPR KFR as a fixed hierarchy. The intended model is different: → →
KPR and KFR are reusable semantic entities that connect directly to applicable KCRs. A principle may apply to 
several concepts; a function may act on several concepts; a concept may have multiple applicable principles 
and functions. The relationship direction is stored canonically, but reverse traversal is allowed at query time.
Canonical relationship pattern
(:KnowledgePrinciple {id: 'KPR_...'})-[:APPLIES_TO_KCR]->(:KnowledgeConcept {id: 'KCR_...'})
(:KnowledgeFunction {id: 'KFR_...'})-[:ACTS_ON_KCR]->(:KnowledgeConcept {id: 'KCR_...'})
DESIGN RULE Do not materialise duplicate inverse edges merely for convenience. The application may 
traverse a stored edge in either direction while preserving one canonical assertion.

RESEARCH RELEVANCE The relationship catalogue is intentionally sparse compared with unrestricted 
semantic graphs. Missing edges are preferable to weak edges because every asserted edge can affect 
recommendation eligibility and explainability.
7. Ontology constraints and graph governance
Constraint Requirement
Canonical identity One distinct reusable entity has one stable canonical 
record. Names may change; permanent IDs remain stable 
unless entity identity changes.
No forced relationship population A relationship is created only when semantically justified. 
Blank relationship fields are valid.
No discipline duplication Reusable KCR/KPR/KFR/SCR entities should not be 
duplicated only because the same concept appears in 
another discipline.
Source fidelity Course descriptions, objectives and outcomes preserve the 
source curriculum. Enrichment does not silently replace 
source wording.
Definition/scope separation Definition states what an entity is; Scope states boundaries 
and exclusions. This is essential for semantic retrieval and 
expert review.
Semantic-description discipline Semantic text is rich enough for embeddings but must not 
be padded with unrelated SDGs or applications.
Lifecycle semantics Deprecated entities remain resolvable through explicit 
redirect relationships such as MERGED_INTO.
No implicit framework equivalence Goal-linked ESD or Curriculum Framework records are not
equivalent to SCRs and are not promoted to canonical SCR 
relationships merely because they are semantically 
similar.
8. Identifier and versioning scheme
Stable identifiers serve the same role that URIs play in well-governed data systems: they allow entities and

9. Sustainability and educational-framework ontology
SCR is intentionally independent of the Sustainable Development Goals. An SCR represents a sustainability 
idea or mechanism; an SDG target or indicator is an external policy/framework reference. This separation 
prevents SDG titles from becoming substitutes for disciplinary reasoning.
KEY DISTINCTION SCR answers 'what sustainability concept is relevant here?'. SDG/ESD/CF answer 
'how can that eligible concept be aligned to global sustainability and educational outcomes?'.
The structural framework path is:

UNESCO ESD learning objectives are selected after the relevant SDG Goal is established. Cross-cutting ESD

objective-to-competency mappings. Similarly, Curriculum Framework competencies may enrich a saved 
LearningDesign without inventing row-level canonical mappings when the source framework does not 
provide them.

11.1 Why the semantic layer moved to MiniLM
Earlier recommendation discovery used a transparent but hand-weighted lexical/character-overlap score: 
trigger-word overlap 0.40, mechanism-word overlap 0.20, concept/keyword overlap 0.10, trigger character n￾gram 0.20 and mechanism character n-gram 0.10, with a dynamic candidate threshold of max(0.055, 0.70 × top 
score). That logic was useful for early diagnostics but required vocabulary-specific tuning and did not provide 
a reusable dense sentence representation for paraphrases or cross-level semantic entry.
The reference semantic layer therefore moved to sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (384 dimensions). 
The design reason was semantic generalisation and maintainability—not an observed raw-score victory over 
the earlier logic. On the preserved 15-case comparison, the reconstructed v2A output had Required 
Top-1/Top-3/forbidden-Top-1 at 15/15, while the initial MiniLM v2B run achieved 11/15 Top-1, 13/15 Top-3 and 
12/15 legacy overall. Those regressions exposed repository/context interactions and led to the v2C semantic￾repository and v3 context/mechanism corrections.
DESIGN CONSEQUENCE MiniLM is retained as a semantic retrieval/ranking component inside the hybrid 
architecture. Ontology scope, typed evidence, mechanism compatibility and guardrails remain authoritative. A 
high embedding score can suggest a candidate; it cannot establish ontology truth or technical eligibility.
